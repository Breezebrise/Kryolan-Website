Why olive skin is so hard to match

Olive skin has a green or grey-green cast on top of a warm, neutral or cool base. Most foundation ranges are built on two axes, pink to yellow and light to deep, so olive gets squeezed in as "neutral" or "warm." Those shades sit on top of olive skin and turn orange or ashy by midday.

When brands do make olive, they usually add grey or green pigment. Done well, it disappears. Done badly, it looks muddy on lighter olive skin. And many brands only make olive in the medium range, so light and deep olive skin get nothing.

What works on olive: muted, earthy colors and deep jewel tones. Olive, khaki, teal, emerald, plum, burgundy, terracotta, bronze and warm taupe. For lips: brick, rosewood, berry and warm mauve. For reds: a slightly brown or brick red reads richer than a pure orange or cool pink red.

What fights olive: pastel pink, icy lavender, orange-yellow brights and frosty finishes. They make the green in olive skin stand out and the skin look grey.

Fix an olive mismatch with what you have: if a foundation turns orange, mix in a drop of a neutral or olive shade. If it looks pink and chalky, a little yellow corrector under it helps. Kryolan Ultra Foundation and Supracolor have dedicated olive families, OB, Olive and SPLO, that mix into almost any base.
